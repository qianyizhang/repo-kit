# Progressive technical walkthroughs

Use for connected lessons or a mechanism followed across several steps. Use
`show-me` for evidence/view selection and [UI/UX](ui-ux.md) for interface behavior.
An **encounter** is a question, concrete case, meaningful action, observable
consequence, and way onward or back.

## Five rules

1. **Show the whole; bound the action.** Reveal how a substantial system fits
   together through an overview, exploded view or grouped outcomes. Give one
   primary action; disclose controls for the current question. A tiny isolated
   demo loses the big picture. Equally prominent knobs recreate the original load.
2. **Define by role before use.** Point to each necessary term and say what it does
   just before its first control or claim. Keep names consistent across text,
   marks and controls; briefly reintroduce the role in new contexts. Disclose exact
   source names/background separately. Equal positions or values can have different roles.
3. **Make the consequence visible.** Say what the action reveals. Change one
   meaningful input; show what moves, what stays fixed, and why. Keep input and
   result visible together. Compare matching cases, moments, orientation and scale
   unless their difference is the question. Explain expected overlap or stillness.
   A changed caption alone is weak proof.
4. **Reinforce through reuse.** Alternate the whole and useful detail. Apply the new
   concept or control visibly to the familiar stage, then a contrasting case.
   Keep close views stable while scrubbing and locate them in the overview.
   Repeated definitions and text-only recaps cannot replace seeing the relationship work.
5. **Connect routes; preserve the case.** Offer a recommended story, optional nearby
   questions, and a small map of here, the route and useful neighbors. Name why an
   edge matters. Shared encounters keep the chosen route's continuation. Provide
   explicit detour returns. Mandatory linear tours restrict exploration; a full
   graph upfront transfers navigation work to the beginner.

## Extend through shared owners

Use plain data and small functions where several concrete stories need:

- **Curriculum:** encounter identities, routes and named relationships.
- **Session:** subject/scenario, source, time basis, controls and return context.
- **Presentation/effects:** content and views of the selected state, constrained
  by what the source can actually show.

Reuse an encounter when its question and meaning remain the same. Add questions
through content and existing effects; put new behavior in its owning module.
Check that another route can be registered without session-engine edits. Extract
observed repetition; keep domain calculations authoritative. An editor, DSL or
cross-domain engine requires its own demonstrated need.

Carry identity forward. Distinguish source samples, seconds, normalized progress
and domain steps; name conversions and the owner of events. Clock comparisons
should preserve the selected case while changing the common time basis. A marker
convention is not a source-emitted event. Returns must restore a valid
subject/source/time/settings combination, not only a screen ID; specify which
independent experiments keep newer choices. Visits do not establish mastery.

## Preserve evidence boundaries

Identify computation, recorded replay and teaching illustration. Recorded samples
do not imply a live solver; a teaching trajectory is not a clinical forecast.
Unavailable or unmodeled results need an explicit state, not a substitute, zero
or invented result. Retry retains the selected case.

Tie legends and values to marks; pair color with labels, shapes or line styles.
Use 3D for spatial questions and simpler views when clearer. Optional pseudocode
shows **Input → Process → Output** with role names and inspectable values. Put it
and raw values behind named disclosure when the main result is visible without them.

## What worked, what remains unproven

The DCC trials and user feedback support the five rules as design directions.
Functional and bounded browser checks verified matched views, actual sample
comparisons, connected returns, explicit clocks and the shared extension seam.
Inspection caught tiny details needing an inset, clipped labels and lost
comparison context after selection. Earlier openings had too many choices,
isolated examples and text-only reinforcement; a very short trial was insufficient
to judge learning. **Comprehension and transfer remain unmeasured.** Treat a new
domain as a transfer trial, preserving its authority and evidence boundaries.

## Acceptance

Test one representative encounter before repeating its pattern. Then exercise the
whole journey: promised effects, cross-route return, reload, failure/retry,
keyboard and narrow layout. Check terminology, input/result visibility and map
continuity. Keep numerical, visual and learning verdicts separate.

Observe whether the reader can choose a next action, identify what changed and
stayed fixed, explain the relationship by words or pointing, use it in a nearby
case, and resume after a detour. Record assistance and confusion; let them stop.
Clicks, visits, completion and time spent alone do not establish comprehension.
